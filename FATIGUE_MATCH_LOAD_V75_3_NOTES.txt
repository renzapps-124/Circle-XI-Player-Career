CIRCLE XI PLAYER CAREER — FATIGUE MATCH LOAD (v75.3)
Base: v75.2 Training Centre Rework

REQUEST
-------
High fatigue should increase the rate of injuries, and should lower in-game stamina
in proportion to how high the fatigue is.

Before this change the Training Centre FATIGUE meter was almost inert. It nudged
training injury risk slightly (and only above 48%, and only meaningfully on Intense
intensity), and it had no effect whatsoever on matches: the career player kicked off
every fixture at 100 stamina and recovered back to 100 regardless.

THE MODEL
---------
One shared curve drives everything, so the meter reads as a single stat.

  load = clamp((fatigue - 20) / 80, 0, 1)

Below 20% fatigue there is no penalty at all. From there it ramps linearly:

  fatigue | load | stamina ceiling | drain  | recovery | match injury
  --------|------|-----------------|--------|----------|-------------
       20 | 0.00 |          100.0  |  x1.00 |    x1.00 |       x1.00
       40 | 0.25 |           91.5  |  x1.14 |    x0.91 |       x1.21
       60 | 0.50 |           83.0  |  x1.27 |    x0.81 |       x1.43
       80 | 0.75 |           74.5  |  x1.41 |    x0.72 |       x1.64
      100 | 1.00 |           66.0  |  x1.55 |    x0.62 |       x1.85

Helpers: careerFatigueLoad(), careerFatigueMatchProfile() and matchCareerFatigue(game),
which caches one profile per match instance.

1) IN-MATCH STAMINA
-------------------
• The career player now kicks off at the fatigue ceiling rather than 100. AI players
  are untouched and still start at 100.
• Stamina recovery is capped at that ceiling, so the top of the bar is simply
  unavailable while you are carrying fatigue — resting cannot buy it back.
• Recovery is also slower (down to 62% rate) and sprinting burns stamina faster
  (up to +55%).
• The v41 physical-attribute layer, which post-processes user stamina each frame,
  now clamps to the ceiling too instead of a hardcoded 100.

Measured live at 80% fatigue: kickoff stamina 74.5, recovery plateaued at exactly
74.5, and sprint drain came out at x1.422 against a predicted x1.4125 over ~11
seconds of open play per condition.

2) INJURIES
-----------
Match injuries — checkInjury() multiplies the final risk by the fatigue injury
multiplier, for the user only. Measured live with a fixed contact severity: user risk
2.500% at 0 fatigue vs 4.093% at 80 fatigue (x1.638 against a predicted x1.6375),
while AI players stayed at 2.500% in both conditions.

Training injuries — trainingInjuryChance() previously only responded to fatigue above
48%, and barely at all outside Intense sessions. It now starts responding at 30% and
carries a squared term so the last stretch of the meter is disproportionately
dangerous.

  chance per session (fitness 90)
  fatigue |  Light | Normal | Intense
       0  |  0.15% |  0.45% |   2.10%     <- unchanged from before
      40  |  0.40% |  0.70% |   2.57%
      60  |  1.06% |  1.36% |   3.79%
      80  |  1.91% |  2.21% |   5.37%
     100  |  2.95% |  3.25% |   7.30%

Low-fatigue behaviour is deliberately identical to before, so nothing gets harder
until fatigue is actually a problem. Risk still respects the original .001-.09 clamp
and the existing nutritionist / recovery-specialist investment discounts.

Fatigue above 82% can now also produce a severe training injury on its own, where
previously that required Intense intensity as well.

3) MAKING IT LEGIBLE
--------------------
A hidden penalty is a bad penalty, so the cost is stated in two places.

• Training Ground command bar: the FATIGUE meter carries a tooltip, and once the
  penalty is live a coloured line appears under the meters, e.g.
      "⚠ Match stamina capped at 75% · burns 41% faster · injury risk +64%"
  Below the floor it reads "No match penalty at this fatigue level".
• Match HUD: the stamina meter now draws the locked-out portion as a hatched red
  tail, so you can see at a glance how much of the bar fatigue has taken away.

SCOPE NOTES
-----------
• Quick Match is a standalone exhibition and deliberately does not inherit career
  training fatigue — it always runs at a neutral profile. Verified live: Quick Match
  kicks off at 100 with a 0% locked tail, a career fixture at 74.5 with 26%.
• Training drills keep their own independent per-drill energy. Fatigue was not applied
  there on purpose: training is what generates fatigue, so feeding it back into drill
  energy would compound into a spiral. Say the word if you want that too.

ALSO FIXED: STALE DEFERRED TRAINING CALLBACKS (found while regression testing)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The 40-drill regression turned up an intermittent crash in Weak Foot Development:

  TypeError: Cannot read properties of undefined (reading '0')
      at spawnControlledTarget (game.js:5486)
      at recordTrainingAction

Cause: closing a drill does not cancel timeouts that have already been scheduled.
Every deferred callback in the training code guards itself with
`if(trainingGameState===s)` — "is my session still the live one" — except the six
inside recordTrainingAction and one in the penalty countdown, which only checked
`if(trainingGameState)`, i.e. "does any session exist".

So finishing an action in one drill, closing it, and opening another within the
360-760ms timeout window let the old drill's callback run against the new session.
When the new session was a Technical Zone drill (which has no controlled-target index,
because it runs its own scenario system) spawnControlledTarget computed
`undefined++` -> NaN, indexed a preset array with NaN, and threw.

Fix:
  • all seven guards now use `trainingGameState===s`, matching the other 13 sites
  • spawnControlledTarget returns early for any session without a controlled-target
    index, so a leaked call can never touch a scenario-driven drill
  • the preset lookup falls back safely instead of indexing with NaN

Verified with 72 rapid open/start/play/close cycles over the exact drill sequence that
reproduced it (penalties -> corners -> weakfoot -> the three Technical Zone drills):
zero errors, where the same stress reliably threw before.

This was pre-existing and unrelated to fatigue; it surfaced because the fatigue work
required another full pass over all 40 sessions.
